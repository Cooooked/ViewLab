Your headset renders less. You see more.

ViewLab is a Windows VR companion app. It asks games to render a smaller, deliberate slice of the frame — then draws a visor, HUDs, racing cues and live panels into what you actually see. Every part of it is yours to tune, per game.

Render tuning

Ask for less frame. Keep the view you want.

ViewLab changes the FOV and render resolution a game sees — before the game draws, not after. In compatible titles that means real GPU savings, not a post-process crop.

Total or split

One value centres the rendered slice; split mode takes separate top and bottom values, so you can push the picture up, down, or asymmetric for your headset and posture.

The visor mask

A black border drawn directly into the game's eye textures — aperture, apex, bridge and curve all adjustable. It bounds what reaches your eyes at zero GPU cost.

Per-game profiles

Global settings plus per-application overrides. A low narrow slice for iRacing, a centred view for DCS or MSFS, a taller one for SkyrimVR — each remembered automatically.

Inside the headset

An overlay suite that lives in the frame

Everything below is drawn into the submitted frame itself — visible in the headset and in ViewLab's own capture source. Each element can be shown, hidden, moved and tuned independently.

Performance HUD & trace

GPU, CPU, frametime and app metrics with alarm thresholds, plus a session graph browser for diagnosing stutter after the fact.

Clock & session timer

Multiple clock modes and a session timer, positionable anywhere in view.

Notifications

Desktop notifications and music track-change cards surface inside the headset without breaking flow.

VR panels

Any window. Any monitor. Inside your headset.

ViewLab can capture program windows — or an entire monitor — and present them as live panels floating in VR. Arrange named layouts, pin them in space or let them follow your head, and edit them with a mouse without taking the headset off.

Layout

Illustrative layout — named arrangements snap panels into place; in VR you drag them with the mouse.

Real sources, real pixels

Panels capture the actual desktop output at a paced frame rate and only re-render when pixels change — static content costs nothing. Failed captures retry; resized sources recover.

Layouts and placement

Named panel layouts with fixed or head-following placement, configurable mouse editing, and in-VR guidance for selection. Import read-only window sources from tools like OpenKneeboard.

Good to know

Questions, answered

Does it cost anything?

ViewLab is free and open source. If it earns its place in your setup, a coffee keeps development going.

Which headsets and games does it work with?

Windows VR headsets on supported runtimes, across games and apps that honour standard runtime settings. Per-application profiles handle the exceptions automatically.

Does cropping hurt image quality?

The render slice is drawn at the game's own resolution — you're asking for less frame, not stretching a smaller one. The visor mask is drawn into the frame at zero GPU cost.

Can I still play normally?

Yes — disable ViewLab or switch a profile and the game runs untouched. Profiles apply per application, so a racing profile never leaks into a flight sim.

What about streaming or recording?

Media Capture is a dedicated source that sees the finished frame — overlays, post-processing and panels included — so your audience gets the same picture you do.

Is my data collected?

No telemetry leaves your machine. ViewLab is local software; the only network traffic is what you choose to stream yourself.

Your headset renders less. You see more.

ViewLab is a Windows OpenXR layer and settings app. It asks games to render a smaller, deliberate slice of the frame — then draws a visor, HUDs, racing cues and live panels into what you actually see. Every part of it is yours to tune, per game.

Render tuning

Ask for less frame. Keep the view you want.

ViewLab changes the FOV and render resolution reported through OpenXR — before the game draws, not after. In compatible titles that means real GPU savings, not a post-process crop.

Total or split

One value centres the rendered slice; split mode takes separate top and bottom values, so you can push the picture up, down, or asymmetric for your headset and posture.

The visor mask

A black border drawn directly into the game's eye textures — aperture, apex, bridge and curve all adjustable. It bounds what reaches your eyes at zero GPU cost.

Per-game profiles

Global settings plus per-application overrides. A low narrow slice for iRacing, a centred view for DCS or MSFS, a taller one for SkyrimVR — each remembered automatically.

Inside the headset

An overlay suite that lives in the frame

Everything below is drawn into the submitted frame itself — visible in the headset and in ViewLab's own OBS capture. Each element can be shown, hidden, moved and tuned independently.

Performance HUD & trace

GPU, CPU, frametime and app metrics with alarm thresholds, plus a session graph browser for diagnosing stutter after the fact.

Clock & session timer

Multiple clock modes and an OpenXR session timer, positionable anywhere in view.

Notifications

Desktop notifications and music track-change cards surface inside the headset without breaking flow.

Sticky notes

Draggable notes in several visual themes — including an HD paper style — for fuel, tyre and strategy reminders on track.

Colour grade & profiles

A post-processing grade with saturation, contrast, shadow and highlight controls, plus three always-available colour profiles switched from an in-headset menu.

LFE spectrogram

A subtle frequency haze driven by a real Windows audio endpoint — think gear-whine and kerb-frequency visualization around the periphery, with optional mirrored copies.

VR panels

Any window. Any monitor. Inside your headset.

ViewLab can capture program windows — or an entire monitor — and present them as live panels floating in VR. Arrange named layouts, pin them in space or let them follow your head, and edit them with a mouse without taking the headset off.

Real sources, real pixels

Panels capture the actual desktop output at a paced frame rate and only re-render when pixels change — static content costs nothing. Failed captures retry; resized sources recover.

Layouts and placement

Named panel layouts with fixed or head-following placement, configurable mouse editing, and in-VR guidance for selection. Import read-only window sources from tools like OpenKneeboard.

Built by a racer

Telemetry that earns its pixels

ViewLab's cue suite was designed around competitive sim racing — information you can actually use at 180 km/h, not dashboard decoration.

iRacing cues

Spotter glow with continuous proximity colour, flag borders, race-start lights, rear-closing pressure cues, grip bar and lap/fuel cards — each independently placeable and testable.

Shift lights

Rhythm shift-light themes including a Guitar Hero–style highway that scores your shifts — PERFECT to MISS — with combos and star power.

Driving assistance

A movable ABS / wheelspin / wheel-lock indicator group using real telemetry — binary ABS intervention plus wheelspin and lock estimates — with fades, thresholds and timed tests.

Out of the headset

Capture what your audience should see

ViewLab Media Capture is its own OBS source. Because the layer sees the finished frame, the capture includes the scene, ViewLab's overlays, ReShade, and overlay layers submitted above it — the complete picture, not a mirror of the raw eye buffer.

Media Capture source

Eye selection, overlay-layer toggle, four crop sliders and reinitialize — stream or record exactly what the headset shows.

Enhancer & Stabilizer

Two companion OBS filters installed from the app: image adjustment (sharpness, saturation, contrast, gamma) and motion stabilization built on LiveVisionKit.

ReShade, remotely

A bundled ReShade OpenXR payload with an in-app ReShade Remote popout — adjust shaders without leaving VR.
